# Write the thing.<br>Publish it online. Keep it yours.

wr1t3r is a app for creative writing, journaling, and notetaking. Plan your days, weeks and months with the built-in planner, and publish the notes you choose on the web with Neocities. Every note is a plain Markdown file in your Dropbox or Google Drive, so nothing is locked in and nobody else holds your work.

[Try it free for 14 days](https://my.wr1t3r.app/) [Buy for $20](buy)

_Works in any modern browser on Mac, Windows, Linux, iPhone, iPad and Android. Works offline once it's open._

--- features

## Plan and write with scene cards on a storyboard

Start a short story, novel, essay, or script with a button and its project folder comes ready with starter notes. Each scene card is its own note. Lay them out like index cards and rearrange them as needed. Stay organized with synopses and status badges, and color-code them by point of view or thread. Switch to the outliner to see word counts and status in a table.

![Storyboard view of a novel's scenes as index cards with synopses and status labels](/shots/corkboard.webp)

## Read the whole draft as one document

The Draft view stitches a folder of scenes into one document you can read and edit straight through. When it's done, compile it to a single Markdown, Word, HTML or PDF file, formatted as a manuscript.

![Draft view showing several scenes joined into one continuous document](/shots/draft.webp)

## An editor that stays out of the way

Markdown with a formatting toolbar, a <kbd>/</kbd> menu for headings, lists, tables and checklists, `[[links]]` between notes that follow renames, footnotes, tags, focus mode, a writing timer and word goals. Grammar checking and dictionary lookups run on your device.

![The editor with a scene open and the formatting toolbar above it](/shots/editor.webp)

## On your phone too

Add wr1t3r to your Home Screen and it opens like an app. Notes sync through your Dropbox or Google Drive, so the scene you wrote on your lunch break is on your laptop when you get home.

--- planner

## A planner for your days, weeks and months

The Today button opens a note for the day: your tasks, a timeline, your habits and a row of buttons for the things you track. A week-ahead note lays out the next seven days, and the calendar shows the whole month. Medicines, health logs and reviews live in the same notebook as your writing, and you decide how much of it you want.

### See the whole month

The calendar page shows Month or Week, and each day holds its events, the tasks due that day, penciled-in plans and stickers. Pencil in a plan you're not sure of yet, then ink it into your calendar when it's firm, or erase it. On a phone it's one week as a list, with the weeks as tabs down the side and a swipe to the next one. Use the built-in calendar, which syncs like your notes, or connect Google Calendar.

![The calendar page for October: a month grid with tasks, penciled-in plans and stickers on the days](/shots/planner-month.webp)

### Plan the week ahead

Each week gets a note with its seven days side by side: the events on your calendar and every task due that day, wherever you wrote it. Tap + on a day to add a plan. At the end of the week, Reflect & recover adds the week in numbers and a few questions to reflect on the week and plan your rest.

![The week-ahead note: seven days from Monday to Sunday with tasks on each, a This week badge and a Reflect & recover button](/shots/planner-week.webp)

### Set it up once, your way

The first daily note asks a few questions: which buttons you want, your daily targets and your medications. Pick only what you'll use. Change your mind later from the planner's menu, or turn any part off in Settings > Features: the health side, the weekly planner or the calendar page.

![The day planner with a setup card above a row of buttons for food, water, meds, exercise and mood, a timeline and two task lists](/shots/planner-setup.webp)

### Track the habits you care about

The Habits card ticks off each habit, counts the ones you do more than once, adds up words written toward a goal and keeps your streaks. Pin it to your Home screen as a tile, small or large. One tap logs a glass of water, a walk, your mood, last night's sleep or this morning's weight. Each log is a line in the day's health note, and the day's totals are saved as properties, so a board can line your days up side by side.

![The Meds button open: today's doses with times, Take buttons, a Take all due now button and as-needed medicines](/shots/planner-meds.webp)

### Medicines, without the guesswork

wr1t3r makes it easy to track your medications, with prescription and dosage lookup from the National Library of Medicine. You can also import a spreadsheet or clinic chart to get started. Mark doses "Taken" with a button click, and add your medication schedule to Google Calendar for reminders even when you're away from wr1t3r. The "chart view" lets you easily see your medication schedule for the day, so you can see what's coming up and what you may have missed.

![The medication chart: each medicine's purpose and schedule with a column for each time of day, and the doses given ticked off with the time and who gave them](/shots/planner-med-chart.webp)

### For the people you look after

Create medication schedules as a caregiver for the people in your life. wr1t3r keeps these separate from each other so nothing gets confused.

![Mom's medicines open from her own Meds button: today's doses and when each was given, and an as-needed medicine with a Take button](/shots/planner-caregiver.webp)

### Targets worked out for you

Give your height, weight, activity and goal, and wr1t3r works out daily targets for calories, water and steps, with protein, fat, carbs and fiber if you want them. Every number comes from [published nutrition guidelines and peer-reviewed research](/health-data.html). Every button shows how far along you are. Log a new weight and it offers to update the targets.

![The targets step of the planner setup: units, age, height, weight, activity and goal, with the calories, water, steps and sleep targets worked out](/shots/planner-targets.webp)

### Tasks that don't get lost

Keep your Critical Tasks and To Do's as primary lists, or write a task in any note. Each day shows the tasks due that day, so do the week ahead and the calendar, and anything unticked from earlier days carries over until it's done. Add a time to a task and wr1t3r reminds you while it's open on your computer. Connect Google Calendar and those reminders reach your phone too.

![Today's note with a planner showing a timeline, critical tasks and a to-do list](/shots/today.webp)

### Look back on what you wrote

On this day shows what you wrote on today's date in earlier years, on Home and on the day's planner. Mood, sleep and weight puts the last month, three months or year of your logs on one page, with your average sleep on good days and bad ones. At the end of a week, month or year, one command writes a review: your entries, habits, moods, sleep and what you finished, with a few questions to answer underneath.

### Stats for your logs

Book, film, TV and game logs add up on their own: how many you finished each year, pages read, average rating, what you're on now and what's next, in bar, line and pie charts. Pin the numbers to your Home screen as tiles, small, wide or large.

![Stats for a books log: 51 books read, 21,775 pages, a 4.1 average rating, and a chart of books read per year](/shots/stats-books.webp)

### Also on the planner

- **Food and recipes.** Log meals from USDA's table of about 7,800 common foods, scan a barcode, or photograph a Nutrition Facts label to add a food. Build recipes from wr1t3r's connected nutrition databases. Each logged item tracks your calories and nutrition for the day. [Where the numbers come from](/health-data.html).
- **Moods and exercises your way.** Change the choices on the Mood and Exercise buttons to your own.
- **A timeline for the day.** Block out your hours, and add events from your calendar.
- **Everything is a note.** Your planner, week notes, calendar, health log and medicine lists are plain Markdown files in your Dropbox or Google Drive like the rest of your notes.

## Publish a website from your notes

Turn on Website in Settings, then click the globe on any note to add it to your site. Export as website turns those notes into a finished site, in your colors. Publish it to GitHub Pages in one click, and from there to [Neocities](https://neocities.org), Netlify or Cloudflare Pages, which update each time you publish. Or download it as a .zip and upload it to any web host yourself. No server, no wr1t3r account, no monthly fee.

![An exported site: a notebook-style folder menu on the left, an essay in the middle, and a sidebar with a calendar of posts, Ko-fi and Patreon buttons and social links](/shots/site-notebook.webp)

- **Only what you choose.** Notes go on the site when you add them. Everything else stays private in your Dropbox or Google Drive.
- **Two layouts.** A menu across the top, or a notebook-style sidebar with folders that open and close.
- **Your name and logo.** A site title, a logo from your notes, and a footer line.
- **Get paid for your work.** Ko-fi and Patreon buttons in the sidebar, or a link to any shop or tip jar.
- **An archive by month.** A calendar in the sidebar and a page for each month, for notes with a date.
- **Find me on.** Links to Bluesky, Mastodon, Instagram and the rest, plus an email link written so spam bots have a harder time reading your address.
- **Share and reply.** A share button on every post. Link a post to Bluesky and its likes and replies show on your page.
- **Made for Neocities.** Plain pages with links that work on any host, and a preview before you publish.
- **Or bring your own look.** Export plain HTML pages that link your `main.css`, so they match a site you already have.

### One button, right in the toolbar

The globe lights up when the note you're on is part of your site. Click it to add or remove the note, or to open Export as website, see the whole site, and publish or download it.

![The globe button in the toolbar, open, with the note's place on the website and Export as website](/shots/publish.webp)

## Questions

### Can I use wr1t3r for free?

Yes. You can put your own copy online for free with Cloudflare or GitHub Pages and keep your notes in your Dropbox. It takes about 20 minutes and no coding. [Here's how, step by step](/self-host.html). The $20 version is for people who'd rather skip the setup: it works right away, updates itself, and works with Google Drive too.

### Do I need a Dropbox or Google account?

You need Dropbox or Google Drive to keep your notes in, and a free account of either is plenty for text notes. In Dropbox, wr1t3r only gets access to its own app folder, not the rest of your Dropbox.

### What does wr1t3r do with my Google account?

Only what you turn on. If you keep your notes in Google Drive, wr1t3r saves them in a "wr1t3r" folder there and can see only the files it made, nothing else in your Drive. If you connect Google Calendar, your events show in the agenda, new events you add go to your calendar, and task and medicine reminders can go there as events so your phone's Calendar app reminds you. Everything goes straight between your browser and Google; I never see or keep any of it. The [privacy policy](/privacy.html) has the details.

### Does it work offline?

Yes. Once wr1t3r has opened on a device, your notes are kept there too. Edits made offline sync the next time you're connected. If the same note was changed in two places, wr1t3r keeps both copies rather than losing either.

### What happens when the trial ends?

wr1t3r asks for a license key. Your notes stay in your Dropbox or Google Drive the whole time, readable by any text editor, whether you buy or not.

### Is there support?

Yes. Email me at [hello@wr1t3r.app](mailto:hello@wr1t3r.app) with questions about how features work, bugs you experience, or your license/billing. I'm a librarian by day, so I'm happy to help where I can. I make wr1t3r myself and use it every day, so problems get fixed and fixes reach your copy on their own. wr1t3r ships as a complete product rather than on a long road map; new features may land as I think of them, and ideas are always welcome, but new features aren't promised.

### Do I need a web host to publish?

You need somewhere for it to live, and free ones work. A free GitHub account gives you one-click publishing to GitHub Pages, and Neocities can be updated from there too. Or download the .zip and drag the files into a free [Neocities](https://neocities.org) site's uploader. Free Neocities accounts don't take audio or video files, so wr1t3r leaves those out unless you ask for them.

### Can I get a refund?

Try it free first. If it isn't working out after you buy, ask within 30 days for a full refund.
